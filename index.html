Why these exist

The documentation tells you
what a setting does.

It rarely tells you what happens when that setting is wrong — and wrong is the state you're in at 3am, looking at a pipeline that ran successfully and produced the wrong numbers.

What you get from the docs

“Photon accelerates SQL workloads and requires no code changes.”

True, and useless at the moment you need it. It doesn't tell you that a single UDF makes Photon hand execution back to Spark, silently, while the DBU premium keeps being charged.

You leave knowing the feature exists.

What you get from the guide

“Photon is a bet, not a free upgrade.”

Then: exactly which operators are covered, what causes a fallback, how to read the Photon task-time percentage, and a two-run test that answers whether it earns its premium on your workload — in an afternoon.

You leave knowing what to do on Monday.

That's the whole method. Find the one sentence that makes a feature predictable, then document every way it goes wrong — symptom, cause, fix — because that's the shape the knowledge actually has when you need it.

Why is Auto Loader free?

Because the fastest way to find out whether these are useful to you is to read one. It's the complete guide, not a sample. If it doesn't earn a place in your bookmarks, nothing else here will either.

What format are the guides?

Print-ready A4 PDF, typeset for reading on screen or paper. Custom vector diagrams throughout, and a cheat sheet on the last page designed to be printed on its own.

Isn't this all in the Databricks documentation?

The facts are, mostly. What isn't is the organisation: one idea that makes a feature predictable, the failure modes collected in one place with fixes, and the trade-offs stated plainly. These guides are the thing you wanted when the docs told you what a setting does but not what happens when it's wrong.

How current are they?

Each guide carries an edition number and notes runtime requirements and preview status where they matter. Databricks moves quickly, so guides are revised — and revisions are free if you bought the guide, the bundle or the library.
